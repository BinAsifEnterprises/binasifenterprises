INSIGHTS · SHORT-FORM VIDEO2 min read

5 reels every local business should post

Short video is often the first way a new customer meets your business. The good news: you do not need a studio, an expensive camera or acting skills. A phone, decent light and your real workplace are enough. Here are five simple reel ideas any local business can film.

1. The welcome reel

Stand at your door, counter or workshop bench and say hello. Tell people who you are, what you sell or do, and where to find you. It sounds basic, but most customers have never seen the person behind the business — and people feel more comfortable buying from a face they recognise.

2. The behind-the-scenes reel

Show the work before it reaches the customer: ingredients being prepared, a shelf being restocked, a style in progress, a repair on the bench. Everyday process footage feels honest because it is honest, and it quietly answers the question every customer has — do these people know what they are doing?

3. The close-up reel

Pick one product, dish or finished job and film it up close, slowly. Good light and a steady hand matter more than editing tricks. Close-ups let people judge the quality for themselves, which is exactly what they are trying to do before they visit, book or order.

4. The quick tip reel

Share one small piece of advice from your trade: how to keep flowers fresh for longer, how to choose the right size, what to check before booking a service. Tips mark you out as the expert in your area, and they give people a reason to follow you even when they are not buying today.

5. The question reel

Take a question customers ask you all the time and answer it on camera, in plain words. You already give that answer several times a week. Recording it once means it can keep answering for you — and it is often the video a new customer watches right before getting in touch.

Keep it simple, keep it going

You do not need all five in a single week. Film one, post it, and work through the set over time with new products, new work and new questions. And if filming and editing take time you simply do not have, that is exactly the kind of work we handle for local businesses.

INSIGHTS · CONSISTENCY2 min read

Why consistent posting beats chasing viral

Every business owner has seen it: one post takes off, the phone lights up for a day, and then everything goes quiet again. Chasing that moment feels exciting, but it is a poor plan. A steady posting rhythm does the quieter, more valuable work.

Viral is a lottery, not a strategy

A viral post depends on timing, luck and an algorithm you do not control. You cannot schedule it, repeat it on demand or build a month of business around it. A sudden spike also tends to reach people far outside your area — people who will never walk through your door or place an order.

Consistency is how trust is built

Customers rarely buy the first time they see a business. They buy after seeing it a few times and getting a feel for it: the quality of the work, the tone, the people behind it. Regular posts create that familiarity. Each one is a small reminder that you are open, active and doing good work.

A rhythm also makes you better

Posting regularly gives you practice and honest feedback. You learn which products people ask about, which videos they watch to the end and which offers earn replies. That learning builds up over time. A business that posts every week keeps getting sharper; a business waiting for one perfect post learns nothing in between.

What a workable rhythm looks like

It does not need to be daily. A few posts a week, at roughly regular times, covering a simple mix — your work, your products, your people, your answers to common questions — is enough to stay present in people's minds. The best schedule is the one you can still be keeping three months from now.

The bottom line

If keeping that rhythm is the hard part, it usually is not a creativity problem — it is a time problem. Planning, filming and publishing on schedule, week after week, is the core of social media management. It is unglamorous work, and it is the work that builds a presence customers can rely on.

INSIGHTS · CREATIVE CHOICES2 min read

Human content vs AI content: when to use each

AI tools can now generate images, video and voice in minutes, and plenty of businesses are asking the same question: should we still film real content? The honest answer is that the two do different jobs — and used in the right places, they make each other stronger.

Where real, human content wins

Anything that depends on trust belongs on camera. Your premises, your team, your hands doing the work, a customer question answered in your own voice — these things prove you are real, local and accountable. For services people invite into their homes, or trust with their appearance, health or money, real footage carries a weight that generated imagery simply cannot.

Where AI content genuinely helps

AI is useful where a camera cannot easily go: visualising a product that does not exist yet, creating a concept visual for an idea you are testing, building an eye-catching backdrop for a promotion, or producing a clean voiceover when recording conditions are poor. It is also a fast, low-cost way to try an idea before spending money on a full shoot.

A simple way to mix them

Here is a practical rule: let real content carry your identity, and let AI content carry your imagination. Film the people, the place and the finished work. Use AI for concepts, mock-ups, supporting visuals and extra versions of ideas that already work in real footage. Keep your branding — colours, logo, tone — consistent across both, so everything still looks and sounds like one business.

One honest caution

Audiences are getting better at spotting content that feels artificial. If a post could mislead someone about what your product, food or premises actually look like, do not post it. Trust is the whole point of being on social media at all — content that wins a click but costs trust turns out expensive in the long run.

How we approach it

Our own approach is human-led: real footage and real creative judgment first, AI tools where they genuinely add something a camera cannot. Neither extreme — all AI or no AI — serves a small business as well as a deliberate mix chosen post by post.

INSIGHTS · POSTING RHYTHM2 min read

How often should your business post on social media?

It is usually the first question a business owner asks us: how often should we post? The honest answer is that there is no magic number. The best posting frequency is the one your business can keep up every week without burning out — because long gaps hurt more than a modest schedule ever will.

Consistency beats volume

Three good posts a week, every week, build more trust than ten posts in one busy week followed by a month of silence. Customers notice rhythm. A page that shows up regularly looks open, active and worth dealing with; a page that keeps disappearing looks closed, even when it is not. When you are unsure, choose the smaller schedule you can actually keep.

A simple weekly rhythm

Here is a pattern most busy small businesses can hold. One useful tip post — a small piece of advice from your trade that helps a customer. One behind-the-scenes or product post, showing the real work or the thing itself. And one reel, filmed on your phone, because short video is often the first way new people meet your business. On the days in between, add stories when — and only when — there is something real to show.

Batch it

Creating something new every single day is exhausting, and it is the fastest route to giving up altogether. Instead, sit down once a week and make the whole week's content in one sitting: write the captions, choose the photos, film the reel. One focused session replaces the daily scramble, and the work usually comes out better for it.

Reuse across platforms

One good idea does not have to live in just one place. A tip can become a reel, then a still post, then a story later in the week. That is the same work, seen in more places, by more of the people who might buy from you. You are not repeating yourself — you are making your effort count.

Watch the right signals

Likes are easy to collect and easy to overrate. Pay closer attention to replies, saves, shares and enquiries — the signals that show a post actually moved someone to act. Notice which of your posts earn those, and quietly do more of that kind. Over time, let your own customers, not guesswork, shape the rhythm.

Keep it simple, keep it going

None of this needs to be perfect. A simple rhythm, kept week after week, will carry a small business further than any burst of enthusiasm followed by silence.
